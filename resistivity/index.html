Electrical Resistivity Surveys (VES) in Aylesbury

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

DETAILS →

Aylesbury's expansion from a Saxon market town into a major Buckinghamshire centre has pushed development onto increasingly varied ground. The town's historic core sits on Gault Clay, but modern estates now stretch onto the Lower Chalk and patchy Quaternary deposits of the Vale. These transitions matter for construction. A foundation that works on the shallow gravels near the town centre may be entirely unsuitable 800 metres east where the Kimmeridge Clay outcrops. Electrical resistivity testing (VES) provides the subsurface continuity that boreholes alone cannot offer. It maps how strata thickness changes across a site. It identifies buried channels. It pinpoints where the water table sits within the clay. For projects around the Aylesbury Vale, combining resistivity profiles with targeted CPT testing gives engineers a layered understanding of ground behaviour before a single pile is driven.

A 2D resistivity line across a site in Aylesbury can reveal a buried chalk pinnacle or a silt-filled hollow that ten boreholes might miss entirely.

Approach and scope

The Kellaways Sand and Oxford Clay sequence on the eastern edge of Aylesbury creates a sharp resistivity contrast that VES reads with precision. Sand and gravel lenses within the clay show high resistivity. Saturated clay shows very low values. This contrast reveals exactly where bearing strata pinch out and where groundwater accumulates. The survey uses a Schlumberger array with electrode spacings that reach effective depths between 20 and 80 metres, depending on site access. Data inversion produces a 1D layered model at each sounding point. Multiple soundings along a line build a 2D pseudo-section. The method works well in urban Aylesbury because it needs no boreholes, no heavy plant, and creates zero vibration. For brownfield sites with unknown fill, we often recommend in-situ permeability testing alongside resistivity to verify the drainage potential of mapped sand bodies. Our equipment logs apparent resistivity values from 0.2 ohm-metres in wet clay to over 100 ohm-metres in dry chalk. All surveys follow BS 5930:2015+A1:2020 procedures for geophysical ground investigation.

Site-specific factors

The most common mistake on Aylesbury sites is assuming the Gault Clay is continuous and uniform. It is not. The clay surface is irregular. It has been eroded into hollows that later filled with soft alluvium. A foundation designed on the assumption of level clay can experience differential settlement if it bridges these buried features. Resistivity surveys detect the hollows and their fill before excavation begins. Another frequent error involves depth to water. On sloping sites towards the Chilterns, perched water tables form within the clay weathering zone. They saturate the upper two metres. Builders who skip a resistivity survey often discover this water during trenching in February. The result is a costly dewatering programme that proper ground investigation would have predicted. A resistivity profile costs a fraction of an emergency sump pump operation and keeps the programme on track.

Related technical services

01

Single VES Sounding

One vertical electrical sounding to determine depth to bedrock, clay thickness, or water table depth at a specific location. Suitable for single-building plots and small extensions where one representative ground model is sufficient.

02

2D Resistivity Profiling

Multiple soundings along a transect with merged inversion to produce a continuous cross-section. Ideal for road alignments, pipeline routes, and sites where lateral changes in the Gault Clay or Chalk surface must be mapped.

03

Grid Resistivity Mapping

An array of VES points on a regular grid across the full site. We deliver iso-resistivity contour maps at multiple depth slices. Used for large commercial developments, quarry assessments, and groundwater resource studies in the Vale.

Q&A

How much does an electrical resistivity survey cost for a typical Aylesbury building plot?

For a single-building plot requiring two to four VES soundings and a basic 2D profile, costs typically range from £440 to £710. The final figure depends on electrode spread length, site access, and whether we need to clear vegetation for the cable runs. We provide a fixed-price quote after reviewing the site plan and the specific depth of investigation required.

What is the difference between resistivity surveying and a trial pit investigation?

A trial pit exposes the top four metres of soil directly. Resistivity sounding reads much deeper without any excavation. It detects changes in clay, sand, and chalk layers down to 80 metres. The two methods complement each other. A trial pit verifies the near-surface interpretation. The VES extends the ground model to foundation pile depth and beyond.

Can you survey a site that already has buildings on it?

Yes, within limits. The electrode cable needs a clear straight line for the Schlumberger array. We can lay the spread along access roads, car parks, or between structures. Hardstanding does not block the current injection if we use special electrode contacts. For heavily built-up areas we adapt the array geometry to fit available space while maintaining the target investigation depth.

What does the final report include for a resistivity survey in Aylesbury?

The report contains the raw apparent resistivity curves for each sounding, the inverted 1D layer models with resistivity and thickness for each stratum, and 2D pseudo-sections where multiple soundings were performed. We include a geological interpretation section that correlates the resistivity layers with the known stratigraphy of the Aylesbury area (Gault Clay, Lower Chalk, Quaternary gravels) and a table summarising inferred engineering properties for each identified unit.
